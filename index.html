The Zel'dovich Approximation

Inflation seeds the universe with nearly Gaussian density fluctuations drawn from an approximately scale-invariant power spectrum, P(k) ∝ kns with ns ≈ 0.96. Gravity then amplifies these perturbations, pulling matter into the filaments, walls, and clusters that form the cosmic web.

The Zel'dovich approximation models this by displacing each particle from its Lagrangian grid position by a fixed vector Ψ(q), scaled by a linear growth factor D: x = q + D Ψ(q). As D increases, matter streams out of underdense regions and collapses into overdense filaments and nodes—shell-crossing marks the onset of nonlinearity.
